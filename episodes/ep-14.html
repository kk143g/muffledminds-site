About This Episode

In Episode 14 of Muffled Minds, host Khawar Shahzad sits down with Nargis Khan, the Little Panther of Pakistan hockey, a former captain and legendary goalkeeper of the Pakistan women's hockey team. She started hockey in 1980 at Lahore College for Women, played for Pakistan for 17 years, and gave 45 years of her life to the game, yet she has never received a stipend, match fees or camp allowances.

Nargis tells how she got the title of Little Panther: in the 1983 series against Ireland, playing in cloth leg guards without a helmet, she conceded only five goals from fifty strikes. She talks about the tours that followed, Malaysia, China and Zimbabwe, the Zia era that tried to keep women inside four walls, and the Benazir government that, as a woman prime minister, still did nothing for women's hockey. She also talks about her 1987 trip to England, paid for by teaching tuitions, and about coaching since 1984 and running her own academy, Roha.

The conversation then turns to politics and the state of the game: why she joined PTI in 1996 and stopped supporting it after 2020, the closing of departmental sports, the praise for Maryam Nawaz and the Punjab government, the paper certificate of the 2018 Lifetime Achievement Award, and her proposal for a sports policy that pays players after retirement. It closes with what success means to her, that without financial stability you are not successful, and the Fish Jar question, where she answers that her luck was getting into the Pakistan team and wearing the green colour.

About The Guest

Nargis Khan
Former Captain · Goalkeeper, Pakistan Women Hockey Team · Lahore

Nargis Khan is a former captain and legendary goalkeeper of the Pakistan women's hockey team, and a member of Pakistan's first women's hockey team. She was born and raised in Lahore, started hockey in 1980 at Lahore College for Women, and played for 17 years before retiring in 1997.

She was given the title of Little Panther by the Ireland team after she conceded only five goals from fifty strikes in the 1983 series, playing in cloth leg guards without a helmet. She received the Lifetime Achievement Award in 2018, has been coaching since 1984, and runs her own women's hockey academy, Roha.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Nargis Khan called the Little Panther?

The Ireland team gave her the title after the 1983 series in Pakistan. Playing as goalkeeper in cloth leg guards without a helmet, she conceded only five goals from fifty Irish strikes, and the Irish players were amazed that such a small, quick girl could stop them.

Did Nargis Khan ever get paid for playing for Pakistan?

No. She says she never received test match fees or camp daily allowances to this day, even though camps lasted a month at a time. She played for free out of patriotism, and says the government still owes the players that money.

What happened to women's hockey under Zia-ul-Haq and Benazir Bhutto?

She says the Zia era tried to keep women within four walls and held women's hockey back for 11 years, even opposing the team's kit. Benazir Bhutto, as a woman prime minister, raised hopes, but she did not send the team abroad or give women's hockey special attention.

What does Nargis Khan say about the Lifetime Achievement Award?

She received it in 2018 along with the other old legends, and felt proud, but it was only a paper certificate. She says every player should have got 10 to 20 lakh with it, and cash as well, because a medal or a piece of paper alone is not enough.

What is her proposal for a sports policy?

She asks the prime minister and the chief minister to make a sports policy under which a player who has played international hockey for 10 years gets one crore fifty lakh after retirement, so they can run something of their own. She also wants hockey made like the cricket board, with a finance and endowment fund system.

Who is the guest in this episode?

Nargis Khan, a former captain and legendary goalkeeper of the Pakistan women's hockey team and a member of Pakistan's first women's hockey team.
