About This Episode

In Find Your Clarity #06, host Khawar Shahzad and guest Babur Ayub talk about getting out of your comfort zone and seeing the world differently. Starting from Robert Greene's idea of taking a different route each day, Babur Ayub explains that every creature sees a different version of the same world: a dog lives by smell, a spider by vibrations, and every human has a different angle too.

Babur Ayub tells the story of his own unorthodox path: breaking away from his family's construction business, a jute waistcoat with a gold-plated button, losing his money in a stock exchange crash, selling shirts door to door, and joining ChenOne, where he went from an unwanted visitor to Operations Head Pakistan in nine months. He shows how handling difficult customers, including a lady who threw a shoe at him and a man who complained about a tie, taught him that the customer is king and that a mistake, once learned from, can be resolved.

The episode closes with the timeline of the universe: time began with the Big Bang, and the Almighty sees the whole timeline in one go. Babur Ayub argues that how we conceive God depends on our time, our literature, our background and our training, so the introduction of God keeps changing from age to age, and from person to person.

Key Takeaways

  • We each see a different version of the world: a dog lives by smell, a spider by vibrations, and every human has a different angle too.
  • Most people plan their future on an orthodox model, copying their parents or the people around them instead of looking beyond the set pattern.
  • The same thing has zero value for a labourer and becomes a status symbol for a rich man; value depends on who wears it.
  • Money comes and goes; the one thing to take care of is that you do not owe anyone, so nobody comes to knock on your door.
  • An unorthodox model of your own, built from experiments, is what gets you further than copying a set pattern.
  • A customer is the king: when he shows a complaint, he should get a replacement, not an argument.
  • The guest's tie story shows how a mistake, once learned from and resolved, can bring the same angry customer back to do 80,000 rupees of shopping.
  • You cannot plan your achievements step by step; they are the product of your aptitude and mindset, not of a plan.
  • How you conceive God depends on your time, your literature, your background and your training; it changes from age to age.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does it mean to see the world differently?

Babur Ayub explains that every creature sees a different version of the same world: a dog lives by smell, a spider lives by vibrations, and every human has a different angle too. Taking a different route each day, as Robert Greene suggests, makes you notice different trees, spots and details you would otherwise pass by.

What is the orthodox model Babur Ayub talks about?

It is the set pattern most people follow for their future: copying their parents, grandparents or the people around them. A Chaudhary's circle wants to become Chaudharys, a businessman's children want to become businessmen, and they rarely look beyond that pattern.

What is the jute waistcoat story?

Babur Ayub and a friend made a waistcoat from jute, the material sacks are made of, with one 18-karat gold-plated button and ordinary buttons for the rest. It was meant for Pajero and Nissan owners, not for the market, and the same waistcoat that had zero value for a labourer became a status symbol for a rich man. They sold 60 to 70 of them.
