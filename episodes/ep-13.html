About This Episode

In Episode 13 of Muffled Minds, host Khawar Shahzad sits down with Naeem Wazir, music director, lyricist and the proud son of the legendary Lollywood composer Bakshi Wazir. The episode opens with the famous confrontation between Madam Noor Jahan and Bakshi Wazir: she said a singer makes a song a hit, he said the creator, the musician, does. Naeem grew up in Bhatti Gate's Mohalla Samya, a neighbourhood that produced Ghulam Ali, Shaukat Ali, Feroz Nizami, Muhammad Rafi and many more legends.

Naeem tells his own journey: a passion for the guitar that he chose not to make a profession, four years at PTV from 1989 to 1992, a master's in political science and then in business, advertising at Orient McCann and his own company for 22 to 23 years, and the telefilm Ujala that he wrote, scored and screened for 1,200 people at Aiwan-e-Iqbal. His debut as a lyricist came with his brother Bobby Wazir's film Azaadi in 2016, and he later became a music director with Thodi Life Thodi Zindagi, winning best songwriter for Lambi Judai.

The conversation then turns to his father's memories: how Bakshi Wazir proved his point with Afsha Begum and Menaj ji, the childhood studio visit where a young Naeem predicted a film would flop, Baddo Baddi still sung by nursery children fifty years on, and songs copied in India. Naeem also explains EMI Pakistan's new royalty agreements, his time on the Pakistan Idol selection committee and the Music Challenge auditions of Ibrar-ul-Haq and Shazia Manzoor, why music is a responsibility and not a career, and his father's advice on success: hard work and luck, the two wings of the bird.

About The Guest

Naeem Wazir
Music Director · Lyricist · Son of Bakshi Wazir

Naeem Wazir is a music director, lyricist, script and story writer, and event manager from Bhatti Gate in Lahore's Walled City. He is the son of the legendary Lollywood music director Bakshi Wazir, and the seventh generation of a family that has carried the responsibility of music for seven generations.

He joined PTV in 1989, worked in advertising at Orient McCann and runs his own company, and came to film music as a lyricist with his brother Bobby Wazir's film Azaadi (2016). He won best songwriter for Lambi Judai and served on the selection committee for Pakistan Idol.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who was Bakshi Wazir?

Bakshi Wazir was a legendary music director of the Pakistan film industry, Lollywood, and the father of Naeem Wazir. His compositions include Jaddon Holi Jai Lainda Mera Naam and Baddo Baddi, and he came from a family that has carried the responsibility of music for seven generations.

What was the argument between Noor Jahan and Bakshi Wazir?

Madam Noor Jahan said a singer is a very big thing and a song becomes a hit because she sings it. Bakshi Wazir said the creator, the musician, is the real creator, because the melody is what becomes a hit. He proved it by having Afsha Begum sing all the songs of the film Akhad, every one a super hit, and by having Menaj ji, who did not know Punjabi, sing a Punjabi super hit.

What is Baddo Baddi and why is it still famous?

Baddo Baddi is a Bakshi Wazir composition that nursery children still sing today, fifty years after it was made. Naeem Wazir says the whole family is grateful to Chahat Fateh Ali Khan, who made the song popular again after fifty years.

Was Naeem Wazir a judge on Pakistan Idol?

No. He says he was part of the selection committee of nine people, not a judge. The judges' stage came later. The committee listened to about 150 people in a day, from 8 in the morning till Maghrib. He had the same role earlier on the Music Challenge programme in 1991 and 1992, where he auditioned Ibrar-ul-Haq and Shazia Manzoor.

What does Naeem Wazir mean by music is a responsibility, not a career?

He says there is no dishonesty in music: there is purity, no half sur, and you must stay inside the raag. Today's songs last a week, while old songs are like roti, cooked every day, and the feel has flown out of the new ones. A music director must respect the words, because even a great poem is useless without a good melody.

Who is the guest in this episode?

Naeem Wazir, a music director, lyricist, script and story writer, and event manager from Bhatti Gate in Lahore's Walled City. He is the son of the legendary Lollywood music director Bakshi Wazir, the seventh generation of a musical family, and he won best songwriter for Lambi Judai.
