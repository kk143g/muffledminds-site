About This Episode

In Episode 2 of Muffled Minds, host Khawar Shahzad sits down with his regular co-host Haris Sajid to talk about why silence hurts more than words. They start with the question of where silence is better and where it is not: sometimes it heals, but when you stay silent instead of speaking, the hurt grows, hatred builds in the heart, the communication gap widens, and the issue slowly spreads from one person to your whole life, until you start cutting off from everyone without noticing.

From there the conversation moves to self-analysis, the bug that helps you step back from a conflict, ask where you went wrong, and control yourself the next time. They discuss the fear of what four people will think, the blunt person who says it at once, and the shut-up call that is sometimes needed. The longest part is about parents and institutes: a child who is stopped again and again for expressing his wishes goes silent, and the Three Idiots examples show institutes that only ask for bookish things while a child's real interest is ignored.

The last part turns to cheating and chances: when someone cheats you, going silent and stepping aside without a word harms you and the other person too, so confront once, talk for your own peace of mind, and give chances, but step aside when your words have no effect. The conclusion is the same as in every episode: communication is the key, in front of parents, friends and in relationships, because silence is not necessary everywhere.

Key Takeaways

  • Silence is not always bad: sometimes it heals, and sometimes you need it to observe and think before you react.
  • Staying silent instead of speaking lets the hurt grow: hatred builds, the communication gap widens, and you slowly cut off from everyone.
  • Self-analysis is the way out of a conflict: step out, ask where you went wrong, and you will control yourself better next time.
  • The fear of what four people will think is a hurdle in your progress: first fix yourself, then think about what others will say.
  • A child who is stopped again and again for expressing his wishes goes silent, and the communication gap at home grows.
  • When someone cheats you, going silent harms you and the other person: confront once and talk, for your own peace of mind.
  • Give chances, second and even third, but step aside when your words have no effect on the next person.
  • Communication is the key, with parents, friends and in relationships, but at some point you should also step aside.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does silence hurt more than words?

The hosts say silence lets the hurt grow: hatred starts building in the heart, the communication gap widens, and the issue spreads from one person to your whole life, until you slowly start cutting off from everyone without even noticing it.

Can silence ever be a good thing?

Yes, sometimes. Silence can be a form of healing, and it helps when you step back into observation mode to watch and think before reacting. It becomes harmful when you stay silent instead of communicating, and keep the hurt inside.

What is self-analysis, and how does it help?

Self-analysis means stepping out of a conflict and asking where you went wrong. The hosts call it the self-analysis bug: once you have done it, the analysis comes first the next time, and you can control yourself instead of repeating the same mistake.

Why do parents and institutes crush a child's interests?

Because of society pressure and relatives: a child who expresses his wish is stopped again and again, until he goes silent. The Three Idiots examples show institutes asking only for bookish things while the student's real interest, like photography or a project, is ignored.

What should you do when someone cheats you?

Do not go silent and step aside without a word: that harms you and the other person. Confront once, talk for your own peace of mind, and give chances, second and even third, but step aside when your words have no effect on the next person.
