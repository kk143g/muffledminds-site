About This Episode

Khawar Shahzad hosts a Basant special from a rooftop in Faisal Park, Lahore, with two guests: Najab bhai, who properly celebrated Basant in 2006 or 2007, and Sajawal bhai. Basant is back after 25 years, allowed for three days, and the episode opens with memories of the old festival — no shortage of dor and guddi, mikes on the rooftops, Jashn-e-Bahara, Guddi Ground and Minto Park, and the rival mohalla gangs. The guests compare that Basant with today, when kite string is expensive, the good karigars are gone, and Gen Z barely knows what the festival was.

The middle of the episode is about why Basant was banned in 2006 and 2007 — deaths from chemical and daati dor — and whether the festival can return safely. The guests discuss the SOPs: the 12 o'clock rule, no chemical dor, rods instead of helmets, and the police role, which DIG Faisal Kamran publicly appreciated. They debate whether the event is a publicity stunt or a genuine revival, propose an institute for Basant on the Covid SOPs model, and describe kite-making in Urdu Bazaar, from the mochi's thread to the special Singapore thread and the rising price of a tawa gudda.

The episode closes with old friends — Rana Shahid, who has quit kite-flying, and Shabaz urf Dode, who has died — a rapid fire round on culture, law, tradition, passion and freedom, and the guest's story of breaking his leg twice flying kites, at age six and about eleven. Through it all runs the argument that Basant is a family festival: neighbours back from Defence after ten years, an ancestral home reunited after seven, and a city that waited twenty years to fly a kite at noon.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why was Basant banned in 2006?

Basant was banned in 2006 and 2007 after deaths caused by chemical dor and daati dor. The ban lasted about twenty years. Basant is now allowed again for three days under SOPs, with chemical dor still banned and a 12 o'clock start time.

What are the Basant SOPs discussed in this episode?

The SOPs the guests discuss are the 12 o'clock start, no chemical or daati dor, rods worn by bikers instead of helmets, police checking for illegal dor and patangain, and the Lesco order to keep children away from transformers and daati dor. The guests also propose a dedicated institute for Basant, modelled on the Covid SOPs, with all departments on one page.

What is the 12 o'clock kite story?

After waiting twenty years for Basant to return, the guests flew their first kite at 12:00 sharp, then again at 12:05 — 'bhai 5 minute aur wait kar lo, kuch nahi hota, 20 saal wait kiya, 5 minute aur wait kar'. The 12 o'clock rule is the official start time for the festival.

What is Jashn-e-Bahara and where is Minto Park?

Jashn-e-Bahara was the old spring festival the guests remember from before the ban, with events, music and kite-flying. Minto Park in Lahore, with Guddi Ground, was one of the places they flew kites. The guests say today's Basant has lost the chas-chaske and shugal of those days.

What is the Basant Special?

The Basant Special is a Muffled Minds special episode recorded on a rooftop in Faisal Park, Lahore, after Basant returned after 25 years. Host Khawar Shahzad talks with two guests, Najab bhai and Sajawal bhai, about the old Basant, the ban, the SOPs and what the festival means for Pakistan's culture.

Who is the guest in this episode?

There are two guests: Najab bhai, who properly celebrated Basant in 2006 or 2007, and Sajawal bhai, who remembers it from when he was younger. Chachu is the kite-fighting expert at Faisal Park the guests talk about, not a guest. DIG Faisal Kamran is mentioned for appreciating the festival, not a guest.
