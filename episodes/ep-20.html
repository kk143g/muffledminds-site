About This Episode

Khawar Shahzad sits down with Hasham Saleem, an entrepreneur from Lahore running an e-commerce business, to ask the question at the heart of the episode: job or business? Hasham opens with failure — Jack Ma's thirty job rejections, the KFC story, the ten Harvard rejections — and his own belief that failure is a step towards success, not a dead end.

Hasham traces his own journey: university batch 2011 to 2015, two years in a software house in DHA, Lahore, doing SEO, a job that paid well but gave him no spark, and the decision to leave and start his own brand, Shopse.pk, after a friend taught him about Meta Ads in 2016. He talks honestly about the early years, the support of his brother and father, and why universities teach the degree but not what comes after it.

The conversation then moves into the practical side: what e-commerce really is, white label versus private label, why SEO brings the most genuine customers, the Pakistan versus UAE comparison — licences, tax, bank accounts and buying power — and the trust problems that make Pakistani customers ask for cash on delivery. It closes with his definition of success — continuity — and a rapid fire round.

Key Takeaways

  • Failure is a step towards success, not a dead end — Jack Ma was rejected from thirty jobs, from KFC and from Harvard, and kept trying.
  • Do not rely on one channel: a Google update can drop your website overnight, so SEO alone is not enough.
  • A business with no digital presence is at a loss — even the biggest brands never stop advertising, because people forget.
  • Start with one niche product and make it private label, rather than listing thousands of white label products.
  • In the UAE you need a licence, corporate tax registration and a business bank account — but the margins and buying power are much better than in Pakistan.
  • The biggest lesson is to start a private label brand for the long term, and never panic — good or bad, the time passes.
  • Success is continuity: the work keeps going through ups and downs, instead of quitting when it gets hard.
  • SEO brings the most genuine customers, because the buyer searches for the product himself — Meta Ads second, TikTok last.
  • Accept failure openly, so new people learn from your mistakes instead of repeating them.

About The Guest

Frequently Asked Questions

What is e-commerce?

Buying and selling products online. Hasham explains it through his own store, Shopse.pk: you list products on a website or a marketplace, bring buyers through ads or SEO, and deliver through a courier.

What is the difference between white label and private label?

A white label product carries no brand name, so the same item is sold by many sellers. A private label product carries your own brand name. Hasham advises beginners to pick one niche product and build a private label brand around it.

Is it better to do a job or start a business in Pakistan?

Hasham says both are good, but they suit different people. A job gives a fixed salary and a professional environment; a business gives no ceiling on income, but demands that you pass through difficulties — he calls it passing through a burning coal.

Why does the UAE restrict visas for Pakistanis?

According to Hasham, some Pakistanis took large bank loans in the UAE and returned to Pakistan without repaying them, and fake companies were registered to bring people over on visas and take loans in their names. He says this has made it harder for honest professionals to get visas.

What is the best digital marketing channel for a new business?

Hasham ranks SEO first, because the customer searches for the product himself and is ready to buy; Meta Ads second, because of Facebook's targeting; and TikTok last, because of a 50 percent return ratio and a less serious audience.

Who is the guest in this episode?

Hasham Saleem, a successful entrepreneur from Lahore running an e-commerce business. He started his brand Shopse.pk after learning about Meta Ads in 2016, took his full start in business in 2017, and also works on Amazon and Noon in the UAE.
