About This Episode

In the first episode of Find Your Clarity, host Khawar Shahzad and Babur Ayub take up the question every Pakistani asks: why is Pakistan behind? Babur Ayub argues that Pakistan is not behind at all in talent, resources or achievements, and that the real problem is moral decline: corruption, lying and hypocrisy, while vested interests capture the benefit that should reach the common man.

The episode goes back to 1947 and through the turning points Babur Ayub remembers, from the early years and the nationalisation of industry under Bhutto to the years since, and tests the question against everyday life: the common man is not prosperous, professionalism is missing in offices, and demagogues win votes with emotional stories while deliverables are never discussed. He contrasts this with nations that have fewer resources but no corruption, and with his own story of rising from a common man with no recommendation to country head and general manager.

It closes on ethics and literacy: morality and ethics begin at home, not in religion alone, and Pakistan's literacy rate, 63% and the lowest in South Asia according to the guest, counts people as literate if they can write their name, while educated people behave without ethics. The answer the episode leaves is that each person has to play their own role, starting with honesty and hard work.

We discuss

  • Is the common man happy in Pakistan?
  • Professionalism in Pakistan: bureaucracy vs industry
  • Talent or poor management: what is really missing?
  • We need to learn ethics: morality starts at home
  • Pakistan's literacy rate and what literacy really means
  • Playing your own role: what one ordinary Pakistani can change

Key Takeaways

  • Pakistan has not fallen behind, it has morally deteriorated and become corrupt, while it has everything: seasons, minerals and resources.
  • A country is prosperous when the common man is prosperous, and the common man in Pakistan is not prosperous.
  • We are hypocrites: we lie, and fake news spreads because there is no institute teaching us how to filter information.
  • Professionalism means handling the common people's matters between arriving on time and leaving on time, and in industry it means delivering results, not working unpaid overtime.
  • Corruption is corruption, and a lie is a lie, whether small or big; only a lie told to save a life may be excused.
  • Morality and ethics have nothing to do with religion alone: they start with upbringing at home, and even countries with no religion punish corruption severely.
  • Leaving everything to Allah's will is not the answer: Allah gave us a brain, a body and senses, and Japan builds earthquake-proof buildings instead of accepting that houses fall.
  • Literacy is not writing your name: it is speaking well and giving presentations, and educated people without ethics behave like the uneducated.
  • In short, we have to play our role, and the higher management has to play its role.
