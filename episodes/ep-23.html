About This Episode

Khawar Shahzad sits down with Mutahir Manzoor, a recent graduate of the National College of Arts (NCA) in Lahore, to walk through five years at one of Pakistan's most prestigious art institutions — from the admission race and the first-year bubble to choosing printmaking and figuring out life after graduation. Sara from Hunza joins for one segment to share her own path to NCA.

The episode covers the foundation year, the choice between fine arts and visual communication design, miniature versus printmaking, and the role of criticism and campus culture in shaping an artist. It also takes on the practical questions: how AI is treated in art education, whether artists need drugs for inspiration, and how a printmaker earns a living after graduation.

Mutahir's central point is that NCA is different because of how it trains its students — attendance, critique and the campus itself — and that art is a way of thinking, not just a degree. The episode closes with rapid-fire questions and his advice for students preparing for the NCA admission test.

Key Takeaways

  • Artists do study: Mutahir says the idea that artists do not study is the most wrong idea about art, and studying matters a lot in an artist's life.
  • At NCA you have to work hard to fail, not to pass: just coming to class teaches you something, and attendance nourishes your growth and creativity.
  • Design gives a solution while art opens a dialogue: the artist makes room for the viewer's own perception of the work.
  • Criticism is what makes an artist: healthy criticism from teachers and friends is what pushes an artist to think out of the box.
  • A printmaker needs three to four years after graduation to stand out in the market and make a name.
  • Thinking differently does not need drugs: an artist becomes an artist by thinking differently from a normal person, and that needs no intoxication.
  • NCA's diversity makes its campus culture: students from every province bring their own music and traditions, and the Pahari community owns its culture openly.
  • Art is a lifestyle and a way of thinking, not a job: it can be a hobby, and you can do it at any time.
  • For the NCA test, drawing, creative thinking and the interview carry equal weight: basic drawing is a must, and the aptitude test reveals creative thinking.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is NCA?

The National College of Arts in Lahore is one of Pakistan's most prestigious art institutions, known for training artists, designers and creative thinkers.

How hard is it to get into NCA?

Mutahir describes a tough admission race: a drawing test, an aptitude test and an interview with equal weightage, and many applicants try for two years before getting in.

Do art students actually study at NCA?

Yes. Mutahir says the idea that artists do not study is the most wrong idea about art, and the principal told first-years that at NCA you have to work hard to fail, not to pass.

What is printmaking?

A technique where artwork is made on a plate, inked and wiped, then printed on paper as a mirror image. Mutahir's thesis was in woodcut prints, one of eleven traditional techniques including lino cut, lithography, drypoint, aquatint and mezzotint.
