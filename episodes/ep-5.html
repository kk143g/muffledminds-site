About This Episode

In Episode 5 of Muffled Minds, host Khawar Shahzad sits down with his former teacher Shahid Bhatti, a lecturer at COMSATS Lahore, to talk about AI and the future of human minds. They open with the classroom: students who once asked questions now ask GPT, LLMs and Gemini, get precise answers, and slowly stop thinking for themselves — and why that leaves them stuck the moment they face a real problem at work.

From there the conversation moves through the jobs AI is ending and the ones it is creating, the freelancers hit hardest, and the Builder.ai story: a company that claimed its AI assistant Natasha wrote all the code, while 700 employees wrote it by hand. They cover the Dead Internet Theory, how Google and social media use our data and voice, and why fake AI videos — a bridge collapse his father believed was real — are changing what whole communities believe.

The last part turns to mental health: screen time, addiction, and why universities are not doing enough, before Shahid shares the StepIn app his team built to trade steps for screen time. He closes with his one piece of advice for the young generation — go towards physical activity, one or two hours a day — and answers the fish jar question: are we actually getting smarter, or is it just an abundance of information?

Key Takeaways

  • Use your brain first: AI can support you only up to a point, and if you never use your mind, you will get stuck the moment a new problem comes.
  • Plagiarism and AI help are different things: if you can explain the work, you can build it; if you only copy it, your critical thinking ends.
  • Learn from the root: the seniors who built big companies in Lahore learned programming from the basics, in Notepad, before editors and copilots.
  • A shortcut track has no life and no utility: if GPT does the work, a company owner has no reason to hire you.
  • Jobs are ending where they are also being created: the work that survives is AI-integrated work, and the skills that survive are human skills.
  • Your data is your footprint: even a deleted picture leaves footprints, and your voice, writing and conversations are all being used for recommendations.
  • Verify before you forward: 70-80% of people cannot tell real content from fake, and fake AI videos are now changing opinions and elections.
  • Physical activity is the one fix: one or two hours a day balances screen time, health and mental health, and universities should give it credits.

About The Guest

Shahid Bhatti
Lecturer at COMSATS Lahore

Shahid Bhatti is a lecturer at COMSATS Lahore who supervises final-year projects, and a teacher host Khawar Shahzad calls one of his favourites from his university days. Originally from Multan, he has worked in computing since 2004, bought his first computer in 1999 or 2000, and now also works at Arbi Soft in Lahore, where he built StepIn, an app that turns steps into screen time.

In this episode he talks about how AI is reshaping education and jobs, why students who lean on ChatGPT and LLMs stop thinking, the Builder.ai scam, the Dead Internet Theory, and what AI is doing to mental health, relationships and the next generation — and why physical activity is his one piece of advice for young people.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is AI reshaping education, according to Shahid Bhatti?

Shahid says students who once asked their teachers questions now ask GPT, LLMs and Gemini, and get precise answers — but their thinking process slows down. When they face a real problem in industry, they get stuck, as if someone switched them off. He says the fix is not to ban AI but to use the brain first: learn to think, then use AI in an efficient way.

What happened with Builder.ai?

Builder.ai was an Indian company founded in 2016 that claimed any program or platform software could be built with zero code, through AI, using a code assistant called Natasha. Shahid says that on the back end, 700 employees were literally writing the code by hand. The company raised 40 million in funding, was valued at around $1.5 billion, and had investors including Microsoft and Qatar, before it was revealed that the work was manual.

What is the Dead Internet Theory?

Shahid describes it as the idea that much of what we see online is no longer made by humans: bots upload posts, bots promote them, bots comment and like them, so the whole process is run by bots, and actual human thinking and involvement has ended.

What is the StepIn app?

StepIn is an app Shahid's team at Arbi Soft built to reduce screen time. It uses Apple's Screen Time API: you block your distracting apps, mostly social media, and then earn minutes back by walking — for example, 1000 steps earns 1 minute of app use, tracked through HealthKit. When the minute is up, the apps block again automatically.

What is Shahid Bhatti's advice for the young generation?

Go towards physical activities, one or two hours a day. He says everything else then balances automatically: screen time comes down and health gets better, while without it, mental health gradually reduces. He also says universities should add a three or four credit course for physical health.

Shahid Bhatti, a lecturer at COMSATS Lahore who supervises final-year projects and works at Arbi Soft in Lahore. He is a former teacher of host Khawar Shahzad, who completed his degree at COMSATS in 2015. He is originally from Multan and has worked in computing since 2004.
