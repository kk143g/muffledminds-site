About This Episode

In Episode 3 of Find Your Clarity, host Khawar Shahzad talks with Babur Ayub about rich versus poor — and the answer is not about money alone. Babur argues that poverty is not only in the pocket, it is in the thinking and the feelings too: a person can be financially rich and emotionally, intellectually, spiritually or culturally poor, and the definition of rich has to be a package of all these areas, not money alone.

Babur explains why rich people think differently — they can afford to lose, so they think outside the box — and why the poor get poorer: the poor do not put all their energies and talent into work, while the same body, senses, oxygen and seasons are given to everyone. He illustrates it with his own life: born into a privileged family, he lost his father's money in the 1994 stock market crash, and rebuilt himself by selling custom-made shirts door to door in corporate offices, including the day he met a CEO who was sitting shirtless in his office.

The episode closes on failure and on being rich but poor inside: failure is a blessing if you learn from it — try, fail, try again, fail again, fail better — and real authority is not in arrogance but in kindness and in staying connected with people at every level, from sweepers and security guards to office boys.

Key Takeaways

  • Poverty is not only in the pocket, it is in the thinking and in the feelings too — and a person can be financially rich but emotionally poor.
  • Rich is a package, a combination of multiple areas: financial, emotional, intellectual, spiritual and cultural — not money alone.
  • A rich man can afford to lose the profit for one month and still live; a poor man whose salary does not come for one month sees his whole budget, family system and mental health disturbed.
  • Failure is a blessing if you want to learn from it, and a curse if you don't: try, fail, try again, fail again, fail better.
  • Losing a match 10–0 and then losing 2–0 means you improved — the art of failing better is very important.
  • The emotionally strong person is not controlled by emotions, he controls them: he will not answer abuse with abuse.
  • Opportunities and success go to those who tolerate imperfections and appreciate people, not to those who find fault in everything.
  • Real authority is not in arrogance, it is in kindness and in making good contact with people at all social levels.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does it mean to be rich in this episode?

Babur Ayub says rich is not money alone. It is a package of financial, emotional, intellectual, spiritual and cultural richness, and a person can be financially rich while being emotionally poor.

What is the poverty mindset?

The episode's central idea is that poverty is not only in the pocket, it is in the thinking and the feelings too. A poor mindset shows in not putting all your energy and talent into work, and in being controlled by emotions instead of controlling them.

Why do the poor get poorer and the rich get richer?

According to Babur Ayub, the poor do not translate their energy and talent into productive work, while the rich can afford losses and think outside the box. The same body, senses, oxygen and seasons are given to everyone, so the difference is the mindset.

Is failure shameful?

No, the episode says failure is a blessing if you learn from it and a curse if you don't: try, fail, try again, fail again, fail better. Losing a match 10–0 and then 2–0 means you improved, and the art of failing better is how the journey to improvement begins.

What is Babur Ayub's shirt story?

After losing his father's money in the 1994 stock market crash, Babur started selling custom-made dress shirts door to door in corporate offices. One CEO, who was sitting shirtless in his office in a vest, returned a shirt, and Babur won him back by refreshing, re-pressing and re-packing the same shirt with a special fabric treatment — because the customer is always right.

Who is the guest in this episode?

Babur Ayub, a retail and fashion leader with 25+ years of experience in Pakistan's retail and fashion industry, a lecturer at STEP IADM, a startup mentor, and the author of "Management by Qur'an".
