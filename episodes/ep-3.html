About This Episode

In Episode 3 of Muffled Minds, host Khawar Shahzad sits down with his regular co-host Haris Sajid to talk about the masks of everyday life: why we wear them, whether they are good or bad, and where they lead us. They open with the difference between the corona mask and the real one we put on our face, and how most people walk around wearing a different mask in every gathering, while few people know how they originally are.

From there the conversation moves to why a mask is needed at all: so people cannot misuse you, but also because of insecurities and overthinking about what others will think. They disagree on whether it is a good thing: Khawar argues it helps you deal with different people and switch between your professional and personal life, while Haris says wearing it too long makes you forget who you really are, kills trust, and pushes you towards depression. In between they discuss the double face, the hero in one story and villain in another, bitching among friends, and hurting yourself to avoid hurting others.

The last part turns to the solution: realization comes first, then communicate directly with the person, do a self-analysis, and change step by step, because a river is made drop by drop. Khawar shares his own practice: writing his day down in a diary every night, looking at what he did wrong, and starring the mistakes that repeat, so the same thing does not happen again.

We discuss

  • Why we wear masks in everyday life
  • Is wearing a mask a good thing or a bad thing?
  • People with double faces: hero in one story, villain in another
  • Where the mask leads: losing yourself, trust and depression
  • Professional masking: switching between work and home
  • Hurting yourself to avoid hurting someone else
  • The solution: self-realization, communication and self-analysis
  • Writing a daily diary to track your mistakes

Key Takeaways

  • The mask is needed so people cannot misuse you, but insecurities and overthinking about what others will judge also create it.
  • Wearing a mask too long makes you forget who you really are, and your trust level in front of people gets finished.
  • You might be a hero in someone's story, but on the other side you can be a villain in someone else's story.
  • The solution to a double face is communication: if you feel someone is double-faced, tell him directly.
  • Realization comes first: self-realization is the most important step, and no one is perfect.
  • Communication is the biggest solution, in every podcast: with anyone, with parents, or inside a relationship.
  • Change step by step: a river is made drop by drop, and small daily efforts add up.
  • Write your day down: a diary shows your mistakes, and starring the ones that repeat keeps you honest.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do we wear masks in everyday life?

Khawar says the mask is needed so people cannot misuse you, and because of insecurities and overthinking about what others will think or judge. Haris adds that in every gathering we wear a different mask, so few people know how we originally are.

Is wearing a mask a good thing or a bad thing?

Both, the hosts say. It can be good for dealing with different people and for switching between professional and personal life, but it is bad when you continue it for your whole life: you forget who you really are, people start judging you differently, and your trust level gets finished.

What is a double face?

A double face is when someone appears one way but is different inside: as people say, what he looks like is not what he is. The hosts quote the line that you might be a hero in someone's story, but on the other side you can be a villain in someone else's story.

What is the solution to double-face behaviour?

Realization comes first: you have to know the thing is happening to you, and if someone tells you, take it positively. Then communicate directly with the person, do a self-analysis, and change step by step, because a river is made drop by drop.

What does the episode say about professional masking?

At work, for example in marketing, you have to deal with different people and represent your company, so switching is necessary. But you have to differentiate the points where the mask is needed from the points where it is not, and switch it off when you come home to family.

Who is the guest in this episode?

There is no outside guest in this episode. It is a conversation between host Khawar Shahzad and his regular co-host Haris Sajid, who he introduces as being with him "hamesha ki tarah" (as always).
