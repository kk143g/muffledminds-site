About This Episode

In Episode 11 of Muffled Minds, host Khawar Shahzad sits down with Muhammad Adeel Javed, founder and CEO of Pakistan Citizens Alliance (PCA), a social impact strategist and humanitarian who has spent 15+ years in education, youth empowerment, fundraising and disaster relief. The episode opens with the question that frames the whole conversation: am I living a life that matters, or just a life that looks good? Adeel lost his father when he was four months old and his mother to cancer when he was twelve, and his mother taught him to always be at the giving end, a lesson that became the purpose behind PCA.

The conversation takes on the difference between real and reel charity: flood-relief packages whose promotion cost more than the items inside them, officials who want their photos on hand pumps, and donations that never reach the needy. Adeel explains what an NGO actually is, how PCA works across its four domains, why he tells donors to give only to registered organisations and always take a receipt, and how zakat should be used, given to one person in full, so that next year he is no longer a zakat receiver.

The discussion then turns to Gen Z and parents: mental health talk that has become buzzwords, the missing middle path of the ummat-e-wasat, and why parents are never listened to. Adeel tells the story of the Volunteer Flagship Training Program, the Volunteerathon on International Volunteer Day, the scholarship boy whose parents ran a small food stall and who graduated from LUMS, and the Isar Store renamed after his friend Hashim Raza, who died of cancer at 19. He closes by defining success as living with purpose, creating impact and leaving a legacy, before the Fish Jar quick-fire round on whether Pakistan can ever meet foreign standards.

About The Guest

Muhammad Adeel Javed
Founder and CEO of Pakistan Citizens Alliance · Social Impact Strategist

Muhammad Adeel Javed is the founder and CEO of Pakistan Citizens Alliance (PCA), a social impact strategist, humanitarian and educator who has spent 15+ years uplifting communities through education, youth empowerment, fundraising and disaster relief. He was born in Faisalabad and has lived in Lahore for the last 23-24 years.

PCA is a registered organisation with bank accounts, tax exemption and a Charity Commission certificate, working across four domains: the Education Support Program, the Volunteer Development Program, the Disaster Management Program and the Community Development Program. Its two schools teach around 550 students, its Volunteer Flagship Training Program has trained more than 2,000 volunteers in 50 batches, and its Isar Store gives subsidised rations to needy families at up to 45 percent off.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is Muhammad Adeel Javed?

Muhammad Adeel Javed is the founder and CEO of Pakistan Citizens Alliance (PCA), a social impact strategist, humanitarian and educator who has spent 15+ years uplifting communities through education, youth empowerment, fundraising and disaster relief. He was born in Faisalabad and has lived in Lahore for the last 23-24 years.

What is Pakistan Citizens Alliance (PCA)?

Pakistan Citizens Alliance is a registered non-governmental organisation based in Lahore, working across four domains: the Education Support Program, the Volunteer Development Program, the Disaster Management Program and the Community Development Program. It runs two schools with around 550 students, a Volunteer Flagship Training Program that has trained more than 2,000 volunteers, and the Isar Store, which gives subsidised rations to needy families at up to 45 percent off.

What is the difference between real and reel charity?

Adeel says real charity is help given off camera, while reel charity is help performed for the camera. In the episode he gives the example of flood-relief packages whose promotion cost more than the items inside them, and of officials who put their own photos on hand pumps instead of letting the work speak for itself.

How can I tell whether an NGO is legitimate?

Adeel advises donors to give only to a registered organisation, to check its registration and trust record, and to always take a receipt, so the donation is recorded in the organisation's bank account. He also says never give cash to individuals on the street, because it cannot be verified, and to send donations to the organisation's bank account instead.

What does Adeel say about zakat?

He says zakat is a beautiful system that we have made needlessly complicated, and that we give it wrong: splitting 50,000 rupees among ten people instead of giving it to one person in full, so all his needs are met. His rule is to give zakat to a person who will not be a zakat receiver next year, breaking the cycle of dependence.
