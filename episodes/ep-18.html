About This Episode

In Episode 18 of Muffled Minds, host Khawar Shahzad sits down with Hannan Dar, a stand-up comedian and content creator, to talk about how content creation really works: from reels and roasting to stand-up comedy, and what it takes to build an audience in Pakistan.

Hannan traces his own journey from performing on stage in class 2 and playing the young Nassiruddin Shah in Zinda Bhaag, through news and corporate creative work, to starting his YouTube channel in 2017 and returning to stand-up comedy through open mics. He explains the ECG formula for content, why quality always beats quantity, and how Pakistani audiences and comedians compare with Indian ones.

The conversation then turns to the harder side of the internet: shrinking attention spans, the loss of patience and the ability to disagree and still co-exist, trauma flexing, and mental health, and closes with rapid-fire questions and the Fish Jar Q/A, where Hannan talks about setting boundaries to protect his mental health.

Key Takeaways

  • Making people laugh is the most difficult job, because the audience is of a certain kind.
  • Quality will always prevail: Aamir Khan does one film a year and it goes super duper hit, while Akshay Kumar's quantity came at the cost of quality.
  • The ECG formula for content is evergreen, controversial and growth: make content that is valid yesterday, today and tomorrow, add one controversial piece for a boom, and grow in your own niche.
  • On social media the audience's attention span is three seconds: hook them in the first three seconds, give value in the next two, and give a call to action in the last two or three.
  • Disagreeing and still co-existing is disappearing: people now try to prove the other person wrong instead of proving their own point, and criticism should always be constructive, not destructive.
  • Trauma is not an excuse: real childhood trauma exists and must be separated from small problems, but people will not give you things just because you are traumatized.
  • Talk about mental health with the right person, and with friends who understand: a friend is a friend only if he is a friend, and no friend is better than parents.
  • Keep on creating and stay consistent: make content however you can, and one day you will go viral.

Frequently Asked Questions

How did Hannan Dar start in stand-up comedy?

He first went on stage in class 2, in a school skit, and started performing in 2011 while doing his bachelor's degree. He did theatre, including work at Alhamra, and improv sessions, and later returned to stand-up through open mics like Teri Open Mic.

What is the ECG formula for content?

A formula Hannan learned from another content creator: E for evergreen content that stays valid yesterday, today and tomorrow, C for controversial content that gives your other videos a boom, and G for growth in your own niche and genre. Combining the three makes a content plan.

Why does Hannan say quality beats quantity?

He points to Aamir Khan doing one film a year that goes super duper hit, against Akshay Kumar doing eight to ten films a year where quality was compromised. Cult classics like No Smoking and Andaaz Apna Apna flopped first and were understood later, which he says proves quality content is eventually recognised.

What does Hannan say about patience and attention spans?

He says the audience's attention span is three seconds, people skip videos after a few seconds, and the ability to disagree and still co-exist is disappearing. He links it to Mr. Beast's heavily produced videos and to childhood, when people waited a whole week for a Saturday cartoon.

What is trauma flexing, according to Hannan?

He describes it as the new generation claiming trauma from small things, saying they are in anxiety or depression over minor problems. He says real childhood trauma exists and must be separated from small problems, but trauma should not be used as an excuse, because life will keep you down if you do not get up yourself.

Who is the guest in this episode?

Hannan Dar, a stand-up comedian and content creator. By profession he is Head of Creative and Digital Marketing at an IT solutions company, and he also works in freelance filmmaking, DVCs and short films. He played the young Nassiruddin Shah in Zinda Bhaag.
