About This Episode

In Episode 7 of Muffled Minds, host Khawar Shahzad sits down with Zaina Khan, a survivor of depression and anxiety who rebuilt her life with the support of her mother, friends, and mental health professionals. Raised in Lahore by a single mother, Zaina spent a year locked in her room with depression; therapy and medication, which she says saved her, put her on the road to healing. Now she and her mother are moving to Canada, where she hopes to become a police officer.

The conversation opens with the LGS scandal Zaina watched from the outside: teachers behaving inappropriately with students, and then blaming the students for not wearing a sash. From there it moves to what life means when you are still figuring it out, how bad experiences shape you, and the family story behind her: a father who did not want a girl, a divorce in Canada, and a mother who worked two jobs and fought to raise her alone.

The second half turns to feminism and the slogans of the Aurat March, whether Pakistan is a safe place for women, how childhood trauma shapes the decisions we make, and how parents should talk to their children instead of frightening them. Zaina tells the story of an Uber ride that left her scared to ride again, and closes with social media: its harms, and the awareness and community that helped her realise she was not alone.

Key Takeaways

  • Bad experiences shape you: they are painful, and they are also what teach you how to deal with life.
  • You cannot live without bad experiences; a life of only sunshine leaves you unprepared when something hits hard.
  • Do not think you cannot do it: if you cannot bathe, work or eat today, do it another day; every new day is a new start.
  • Parents need discipline and friendliness together, so children trust them enough to tell them their secrets.
  • Friends come first after parents: 8 of Zaina's 10 friends have separated parents, and relating to each other is what gives courage.
  • Forget but do not forgive: do not forgive someone who wronged you, but do not keep carrying it in your mind either.
  • Complain about a bad driver: staying silent out of fear puts the next person in danger.
  • Social media is in your hands: use it negatively or positively, and decide how you use it.
