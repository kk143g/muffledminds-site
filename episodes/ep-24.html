About This Episode

In Episode 24 of Muffled Minds, host Khawar Shahzad sits down with stand-up comedian and content creator Rao Mubeen to talk about comedy in a country under pressure from inflation, from daily struggles and anxiety to viral reels and live shows.

Rao Mubeen traces his journey from Sindh to Punjab and Lahore, through a YouTube channel started in 2019, Instagram reels, and his first stand-up shows, and explains how observational humour works, why scripted and spontaneous comedy need each other, and what it takes to hold a Pakistani audience that talks through the show.

The conversation also covers roast culture, the Samay Raina controversy and why comebacks are harder in Pakistan, the Gen Z audience, comedy in Lahore versus Karachi and Islamabad, and the rapid-fire round that closes the episode.

Key Takeaways

  • Stand-up in Pakistan is a depression journey: a joke you think will explode can get a reaction of beat this guy, and the pressure is whether the joke will work at all.
  • A comedian's humour is observational: the difference is what new angle you find in things everyone sees but no one has identified.
  • Scripted and spontaneous comedy need each other: groundwork is spontaneous, the stage bit is scripted and tested again and again, and crowd work is spontaneous.
  • Holding a Pakistani audience starts with involving the person who is talking: once he is involved, everyone else follows.
  • Roast culture is risky in Pakistan, but situational jokes about a field are not personal attacks.
  • Stand-up goes with freedom of speech, and in Pakistan there is only speech, no freedom: he has been cancelled from universities for light political jokes.
  • A comeback after a controversy is far harder in Pakistan than in India: here it goes straight to being cancelled from life, especially on religious matters.
  • The Gen Z audience is easy to reach because stand-up is a modern art, but hard to keep: once they have had the fun, they may never come back.
  • If a woman is not safe from your eyes, you are not worthy of being called a man.
