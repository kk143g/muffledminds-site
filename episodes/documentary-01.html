About This Episode

The Earth looks completely peaceful from above, but it never stops, not even for a moment. This first Muffled Minds documentary explains, in Urdu, how the Earth spins silently on its axis, and how that spinning holds the whole story of day and night: when a part of the Earth faces the Sun, day breaks out there, and as that part turns away, the light fades into evening and then night. The Sun does not go anywhere and does not hide; the Earth turns, and the light changes with it.

Then the documentary turns to the seasons. Summer and winter do not come from the heat of the Sun, but from the 23.5 degree tilt of the Earth. As the Earth makes its huge circle around the Sun through the year, different parts receive light from different angles: direct light brings long days and summer, slanted light brings short days and winter, which is why one part of the world can have summer while another part has winter at the same time.

The last part covers the most astonishing phenomenon of all: at the Arctic and the Antarctic, day lasts for six months and night for six months. When the tilted Earth keeps its upper part facing the Sun, the Sun does not set at all, shining 24 hours a day for months, the midnight sun. Six months later that same polar region has turned away from the Sun and darkness covers it for six months, the polar night. It closes with the thought that day and night, the seasons, months-long polar nights and never-ending days are all part of the beautiful, never-ending journey of the Earth.

  • The Sun does not rise or set: the Earth spins on its axis, and the part facing the Sun has day.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes day and night?

The rotation of the Earth. As the Earth spins on its axis, the part facing the Sun has day, and as that part turns away, the light fades into evening and then night. The Sun does not go anywhere or hide; the Earth turns and the light changes with it.

Why do seasons change?

Because of the 23.5 degree tilt of the Earth, not the heat of the Sun. As the Earth circles the Sun through the year, different parts get direct or slanted light: direct light brings summer, slanted light brings shorter days and winter.

Why does one part of the world have summer while another has winter?

Because at the same time, one part of the Earth faces the Sun in direct light while the opposite part gets slanted light. The documentary explains that summer and winter happen at the same moment in different parts of the world.

What is the midnight sun?

At the Arctic and Antarctic, the Sun does not set for months. When the tilted Earth keeps its upper part facing the Sun, the Sun stays in the sky even at night time, shining 24 hours a day for months. This is called the midnight sun.

What is the polar night?

Six months later, the same polar region has moved away from the Sun in the orbit of the Earth, and darkness covers it for six months. The Sun is not seen in the sky at all, day or night, the same darkness all the time. This is called the polar night.
