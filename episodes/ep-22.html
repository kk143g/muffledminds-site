About This Episode

Khawar Shahzad sits down with clinical psychologist Momina Yahya to ask why Gen-Z feels mentally exhausted. The answer starts with the brain itself: it was never built to process information this fast, and 20-second reels, constant comparison and non-stop news leave it overwhelmed — emotionally and mentally.

From social media's impact and shutting down mental noise, the conversation moves to overthinking, imposter syndrome and burnout, then to handling negative thoughts by separating what is in your control from what is not. The second half turns to children: their emotional and social wellbeing, bullying and ragging, parenting mistakes, and dyslexia — what it is, why it goes unreported in Pakistan, and why early intervention matters.

Momina's central point is that emotional regulation is the one thing that can change everything — for parents and children alike — and that asking a therapist for help is not a weakness. The episode closes with rapid-fire questions and the Fish Jar question about acceptance: accept what has happened, and move forward.

Key Takeaways

  • Boredom is the solution: take half an hour with no stimulus — no music, mild or no lights — and just sit, letting your brain process what happened in the whole day.
  • Imposter syndrome comes from perfectionism and an unrealistic standard that keeps moving up: even after achieving something, you feel you did not deserve it and fear being found out.
  • Separate what you can control from what you cannot: your sleep, food, health and study time are in your control, while the economy, wars and what others think are not — and accept the rest.
  • The brain was not built for this pace: 20-second reels back to back leave no time to process, and the brain gets overwhelmed emotionally and mentally.
  • Treat a child as a child: a child's brain is not fully formed, so do not expect adult behaviour from them.
  • Dyslexia is a learning disorder, not laziness: children who cannot read may have weak phonological skills and need assessment, not scolding.
  • Early intervention matters most for dyslexia: the younger the brain, the faster it can learn the skill.
  • Emotional regulation is the single most important thing a person can learn or teach: it ends small fights and leaves you satisfied with yourself.
  • Asking for help is not a weakness: if something is not working, go to the experts and the therapist instead of blaming yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Gen-Z mentally exhausted?

Because the brain gets far more input than it can process: 20-second reels, constant comparison and non-stop news leave no time to process, and the brain gets overwhelmed emotionally and mentally.

What is imposter syndrome?

The feeling of being a fraud despite achievement. Momina traces it to perfectionism and an unrealistic standard that keeps moving up, so even after achieving something you feel you did not deserve it and fear being found out.

What is dyslexia?

A learning disorder in which children struggle to read — they may not learn letter sounds, or may read words without understanding them. It is not laziness, and it needs assessment and early intervention, not scolding.

What parenting mistakes does the episode cover?

Self-blame and impossibly high standards, calling a struggling child lazy, and treating children as adults when their brains are not fully formed yet.

What is the one thing that can change the world?

Emotional regulation. Momina calls it the best thing a person can learn or teach — it ends small fights and leaves you satisfied with yourself.

Who is the guest in this episode?

Momina Yahya, a clinical psychologist who specialises in parenting and childhood-related behaviours and children's emotions. She holds a BS from the Institute of Applied Psychology, Punjab University, and an MS in Clinical Psychology from Punjab University.
