About This Episode

In Episode 8 of Muffled Minds, host Khawar Shahzad and co-host Babur Ayub sit down with Khalid Jamshed, a PCB referee, mindset coach, nutritionist, journalist and writer, to talk about cricket, chaos and what a balanced life really means. Khalid's own story runs from first-class cricket and banking to newspapers, a financial-sector career and then a complete change into nutrition and wellness counselling, with two books on holistic wellness and a novel on transgender people in Pakistan on the way.

The conversation opens with romance and idealism: Khalid's love of books, the dreams of becoming Majid Khan or Imran Khan, and how over-glorified stories from elders, culture and religion clash with reality, through the Audrey railway-station story, Aurangzeb and Babar, and the question of how to raise a generation that can tell the difference. It moves to success, which Khalid calls a process, not a destination, and to peace of mind as a state of balance between emotions, learning, relationships, health and mindset.

The cricket half turns to the dark side Khalid says he has seen up close: match fixing, which he says still happens at the individual level as smart fixing and fancy fixing, the PCB's role, the Babar Azam episode and the avalanche of public opinion, the 7,000 matches planned at school and junior level, the death of club cricket and the grounds it needs. It closes with food and the mind, how what you eat shapes mental health, why religion is sold like a powder by every ruler, and the fish-jar question: what does he want to be in ten years.

Key Takeaways

  • Success is a process, not a destination; life is dynamic and never stops at one place.
  • Peace of mind is a state of balance between emotions, learning, relationships, health and mindset.
  • Give time to family, friends, sleep and exercise: that is what creates balance, and blind running causes burnout.
  • Khalid says the dark side exists everywhere, and match fixing still happens at the individual level as smart fixing and fancy fixing.
  • Compare performance with performance: a talent should be judged against talent, not against prayers.
  • Club cricket was the nursery; without grounds and school cricket, quality players stop coming.
  • You are what you eat: food, taste buds and the reward system shape mental health and decisions.
  • Happiness and positivity are dismissed as nonsense by many, but Khalid says the rat race is what has to end.

About The Guest

Khalid Jamshed
PCB Referee · Mindset Coach · Nutritionist · Journalist and Writer

Khalid Jamshed is a PCB referee, mindset coach, nutritionist, journalist and writer. He is a member of the PCB's elite panel as a referee, a first-class cricketer, a banker and a corporate trainer, and the author of two books on holistic wellness, with a novel on transgender people in Pakistan on the way. He has an MA in English and an MBA, tried the CSS exam twice, and worked as a journalist, columnist and feature writer in newspapers before a long career as a financial-sector executive.

He later changed career into nutrition, completing advanced levels 7, 6, 5 and 4, with certifications from Harvard and LUMS, and took an MSc in Psychology from the Virtual University. He now works as a wellness counsellor. In this episode he talks about how players are groomed, the dark side of cricket and the PCB, what success means, and how discipline, fitness and mindset shape mental health.
