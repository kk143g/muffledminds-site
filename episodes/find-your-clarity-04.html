About This Episode

In this episode of Find Your Clarity, host Khawar Shahzad talks with Babur Ayub about AI parenting: children today ask ChatGPT before they ask their parents, and the question is whether we are raising the first generation brought up by algorithms. The conversation separates two very different things — parents using AI cleverly to raise their children, and parents simply handing a child a phone because they have no time — and it asks what that choice is doing to curiosity, to responsibility, and to the child's mind.

Babur Ayub argues that real AI parenting looks like a mother who asks AI for a five-minute story and then spends the remaining hour telling it herself and personalising it with her child, or a parent who feeds a baby's crying and feeding patterns into AI and gets back guidance on what to check. Handing the child a phone just to free yourself, he says, is not AI parenting at all: it is the modern version of putting a baby in a cradle and letting someone rock it, and its first effect is on the eyesight, with hospitals seeing countless children of three to five whose eyesight is weak. Curiosity, he says, is almost finished, because an answer that once took days now takes seconds: a student only wants his paper solved, and in Pakistan a boy recently asked AI how to get rid of his father and was told which poison to use. AI, the episode explains, only answers from the data of the last ten or twenty years that was fed into it, and through our prompts we are also raising the AI itself.

The episode closes with practical rules: parents should give children right sense, right energy and a right prompt, and teach them how to know what to believe, and busy parents do have time, since the few hours left after work, sleep and meals are most precious when spent with the children. Babur Ayub ends by asking what we are saving time for — microwaves and air fryers finish everything in minutes, yet the time saved goes to more screen time — and says that life belongs to the productive, while a phone that does not help your survival leaves you lost in nothingness.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ChatGPT raising our children?

According to the episode, children now ask ChatGPT before they ask their parents, and Babur Ayub calls this the first generation being raised by algorithms. But he draws a line: that only happens when parents hand the child a phone to free themselves; real AI parenting is parents using AI smartly for their children.

Is giving a child a mobile phone the same as AI parenting?

No. The guest says it is not AI parenting at all, it is something nonsense. He compares it to putting a baby in a cradle in the old days and letting someone rock it, and says its first effect is on the child's eyesight, with hospitals seeing countless children of three to five whose eyesight is weak.

Is AI killing children's curiosity?

Almost, the episode says: an answer that once took days now takes seconds, so a student only wants his paper solved instead of learning. But the value of curiosity depends on the question, and asking who am I, or who is God, is a curiosity that stays a curiosity.

Can AI be blamed for what a child does?

No. The episode's example is a boy who asks AI how to become a king, and a child who cannot tell a court 'AI told me to do it', because AI's answer cannot be challenged and AI has no standing to answer for it. AI only answers from the data it was fed and the prompt it was given.

Will AI take people's jobs?

The episode says new inventions create new jobs, not only take them. The calculator faced the same criticism, and when ATMs came, bank clerks feared losing their jobs, but new departments were created to feed the machines, fix them and serve the customers.

Babur Ayub, who has 25+ years of leadership experience in Pakistan's retail and fashion industry, including Chenone, Hang Ten, Servis Group, Imperial Stores, Khas Stores and Onieo. He is a lecturer at STEP IADM, a startup mentor, and the author of "Management by Qur'an".
