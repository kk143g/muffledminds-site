About This Episode

In the second episode of Muffled Minds Weekly Buzz, Haris Sajid hosts for the first time, with Khawar Shahzad, Hamza and Noorul Huda Khan. They open with Ducky Bhai's arrest and the bribery case around him: the 1xBet and betting allegations, the NCCIA, Sarfaraz Chaudhry, and whether the system or the individual is to blame. From there they debate the judiciary's ranking, the CSS system and hiring, and whether a person should follow the system or their own moral compass.

The conversation moves to the Bondi Beach shooting in Australia, the Indian propaganda that followed, and the Muslim man who stopped the attacker. They discuss Netanyahu's tweet linking the attack to Palestine, the image of Muslims abroad, and whether the attack was a planned distraction in global politics. Then comes the DG-ISPR press conference and Imran Khan's tweets calling the Chief of Army Staff a mental patient, and whether the army should answer a politician at all.

The longest thread is minus one: from Liaquat Ali Khan and Hussain Shaheed Suhrawardy through Sheikh Mujibur Rahman, Zulfikar Ali Bhutto, Benazir Bhutto and Nawaz Sharif to Imran Khan, and why removing a leader never removes the party. They close with Lahore's smog, the EPA, rickshaws and the driving licence test, and the personal choice not to pay bribes, even when it costs seven years without a gas connection.

Key Takeaways

  • Ducky Bhai's arrest is about more than one YouTuber: the hosts trace the betting money, the NCCIA's power and the question of who benefits from the case.
  • A Muslim stopped the Bondi Beach attacker, and the hosts say that single act kept Islamophobia from exploding after the shooting.
  • A terrorist has no religion and no country, but the propaganda after Bondi Beach blamed Islam because of the attacker's name.
  • Minus one never works: removing Liaquat Ali Khan, Suhrawardy, Mujibur Rahman, Bhutto, Benazir or Nawaz Sharif never finished their parties, it only created the next issue.
  • The army is the most organised institution in Pakistan, but the hosts ask why the police, with a fraction of the budget, cannot be organised the same way.
  • Lahore's smog cannot be fixed in five years: short-term measures and banning rickshaws without an alternative only punish the poorest drivers.
  • The driving licence written test asks about signs that do not exist on Pakistani roads, so people pay bribes instead of learning the rules.
  • One host refused to pay a bribe for a gas connection and has waited seven years: the system is designed so that honesty costs more than corruption.
