About This Episode

In the first episode of Muffled Minds Weekly Buzz, host Khawar Shahzad sits down with Hamza and his regular co-host Haris Sajid to talk about current affairs, starting with the Lahore traffic challans and the FIRs being filed for violations like one-way driving. They argue about whether the FIR is fair: the challans alone did not stop people, a 200-rupee fine means nothing to a chingchi driver who earns it back with four more passengers, and the public only gets agitated when the punishment finally bites, even though the net effect, they agree, is positive.

From there the conversation widens to why people break the rules at all: lack of education, a bad day, or the feeling that everyone else is doing it too, and to what would actually fix it, from public transport and signal-free roads to the example of Dubai, where wardens, heavy fines and points deduction make people follow the rules. They also talk about helmets, telling the story of a DSP who distributed 100 helmets after losing his 15-year-old son in a road accident, and about how children learn everything from watching their parents, even a father wiping his bike in the morning.

The second half turns to Karachi, where a three-year-old boy, Ibrahim, fell into an open manhole on a main road and his body was recovered only after 15 hours, and to the minister who told journalists to consider it his favour that he came to hold a press conference. The hosts recall Alamgir's FixIt campaign, which spray-painted Fix It on broken manholes and fixed roads itself, and close by reading viewer comments: the English-Urdu mix in their conversation, the Bakhshi Wazir confusion, and two chit-draw questions about when the nation will start thinking about change and how social media is used for opinion harvesting.

Key Takeaways

  • A 200-rupee challan does not stop a chingchi driver who earns it back with four more passengers; the FIR is what finally makes people notice.
  • People break signals for many reasons: no education, hormones running high, or simply a bad day, and no single punishment fixes all of them.
  • Wearing a helmet in your hand to avoid a challan is not protection; a DSP who lost his 15-year-old son gave away 100 helmets so others would not lose theirs.
  • Children learn from watching, not from being told: a father wiping his bike teaches his son to wipe his cycle without a word.
  • Ibrahim, three years old, fell into an open manhole on a main road in Karachi and his body was found only after 15 hours, further ahead in a big drain.
  • When the state does not fix a broken manhole, citizens can: the FixIt campaign spray-painted the fault and fixed the road itself.
  • Urdu and English are mixed because that is how people actually speak: in routine conversation Urdu is only 20 to 30 percent and English is 70 to 80 percent.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are FIRs being filed for traffic violations in Lahore?

Because challans alone did not stop people. In the last five days before this episode, around 4,000 FIRs were filed, about 3,000 of them for one-way violations, and the hosts say a 200-rupee fine means nothing to a driver who earns it back with a few more passengers. They agree the FIR is harsh but its net effect is positive.

What happened in the Karachi manhole incident?

A three-year-old boy, Ibrahim, fell into an open manhole on a main road in Karachi. Rescue 1122 and other organisations searched for 15 hours, but his body was recovered only later, further ahead in a big drain, by a sweeper. The hosts also discuss the minister who told journalists to consider it his favour that he came to hold a press conference.

What is the FixIt campaign mentioned in the episode?

A campaign started in Karachi by Alamgir, who spray-painted Fix It on broken manholes and bad roads, then built a team that contributed together to fix the road and posted about it. The hosts say the campaign is still running and other young people in Karachi are doing the same with potholes.

Why do the hosts mix Urdu and English in the episode?

Because that is how they actually speak. In a normal conversation their Urdu is only 20 to 30 percent and English is 70 to 80 percent, so they express their thoughts faster in English. They are not trying to prove they are educated; the podcast is a normal conversation, not a purely Urdu or purely English one.

What is Weekly Buzz?

Weekly Buzz is a Muffled Minds series on current affairs, hosted by Khawar Shahzad with Hamza and Haris Sajid. This first episode covers the Lahore traffic challans and FIRs, the Karachi manhole incident, and replies to viewer comments from previous episodes.

Who is the guest in this episode?

There is no outside guest in this episode. It is a conversation between host Khawar Shahzad and his co-hosts Hamza and Haris Sajid, who Khawar introduces in the intro as the think tanks behind Muffled Minds.
