About This Episode

In Episode 9 of Muffled Minds, host Khawar Shahzad sits down with Khaqan Mahmood, director of London School System and a former president of a private school association, to ask why traditional schooling is failing. The episode opens with Albert Einstein's line that everybody is a genius, but if you judge a fish by its ability to climb a tree, it will spend its whole life thinking it is stupid, and Khaqan's own story of how he and his cousin Shavez Mahmood decided one night to build a school.

The conversation runs through the Single National Curriculum, from the 2006 National Curriculum to the 2020 SNC under Imran Khan and education minister Murad Raas, with Punjab Textbook Board alignment, compulsory Quran teaching and licensing through PEPRAS, and the madrasa system left out. It takes on standardized testing, invented by Frederick James Kelly, who later called it cruel, and now running through CSS, MDCAT, ECAT, NTS and SAT, and the Bollywood film Faltu, whose fake university becomes a lesson in skill-based schooling and Matric Tech.

The second half turns to teachers, who are paid as little as a school charging 100 rupees in fees can afford, and to private schools squeezed by licensing, POS, labour laws, EOBI and social security, called a mafia while creating jobs. The budget gives education two to three percent, 2.5 to 3 crore children are out of school, and holidays eat the year, the school open less than 100 days in 365, while home schooling grows in Karachi. Parents close the episode, with the school-teacher-parent triangle, the ICS story, and a final message: do not force your child to become only a doctor or an engineer.

  • Standardized testing: Frederick James Kelly and MCQs in CSS, MDCAT, ECAT, NTS, SAT
  • Faltu University and skill-based schooling: Matric Tech and new matric groups

Key Takeaways

  • Education is not only for a job; it teaches manners, management and how to move in society.
  • Do not judge a fish by its ability to climb a tree: a child's talent decides what he should become.
  • The Single National Curriculum aligned publishers and made Quran teaching compulsory, but madrasas were left out.
  • Standardized testing was called cruel by its own inventor; judging a child by MCQs alone is injustice.
  • Students are not useless: Matric Tech lets a child who cannot do math still earn a matric through a skill.
  • A teacher is like a doctor: one operates on the heart to save a life, the other enters a child's heart to change it.
  • Private schools create jobs and pay teachers well when fees allow, but licensing and departments punish the licensed.
  • Parents must watch their child's thinking, friends and gaps; forcing a career leads to failure, pressure and worse.

About The Guest

Khaqan Mahmood
Director of London School System · Former President, Private School Association

Khaqan Mahmood is the director of London School System, a private school in Kamoke, Gujranwala, and a former president of a private school association. He comes from a Jatt family and studied FSc with his cousin Shavez Mahmood, who now lives in England; the two of them decided one night to build a school that would bring national-level education and the things missing from the system.

He completed his masters before deciding to enter the school field, and he is now doing a PhD in Communication Studies. He speaks in this episode about why traditional schooling is failing, the Single National Curriculum, standardized testing, skill-based schooling, teacher pay, private schools and the role of parents.

What is the Single National Curriculum?

The Single National Curriculum, or SNC, is Pakistan's uniform curriculum, brought in under Imran Khan's government in 2020 after the 2006 National Curriculum. Education minister Murad Raas and his team had publishers like Oxford, Gohar, Afaq and Cambridge align every book to a per-class outline, made Quran teaching compulsory with a full syllabus, and made SNC-aligned books a condition of a school's registration through PEPRAS.

Why does the episode call standardized testing cruel?

Frederick James Kelly invented standardized testing and multiple choice questions, and later said the pattern was so cruel that applying it to children was injustice and it should be stopped at once. In Pakistan, CSS and entry tests like MDCAT, ECAT, NTS and SAT are all MCQs, so a student's whole future is judged by ticking boxes rather than by skills, personality, thinking or understanding.

What is Matric Tech?

Matric Tech is the government's skill-based addition to matric. A child who cannot understand math, physics, chemistry or biology can now take matric in new groups like fashion designing or agriculture, or in computer technology, with 50 percent objective and 50 percent subjective or practical marks, and then go on to diplomas in cooking, hotel management, ticketing, beauty and dermatology instead of failing out.

Are private schools just making money?

Khaqan says calling it just money-making is incomplete. A school charging 2,500 to 4,000 rupees pays its teachers well, and teachers stay 20 to 30 years for the respect and pay. Even LUMS charges 8 to 20 lakh a semester because parents trust what it produces. Private schools also create jobs, his two schools employ 70 to 80 people, while licensing, POS, labour laws and departments punish the schools that do register.
