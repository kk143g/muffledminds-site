About This Episode

Khawar Shahzad sits down with Moeez Haider, an Assistant Transportation Officer in Pakistan Railways, to ask what CSS really is — and what happens after you clear it. Moeez cleared the exam in 2022, after three years of preparation, and talks about the journey honestly: the rote-learning system he had to unlearn, the stress that never leaves you until allocation, and why he believes self-belief matters more than any academy.

The conversation then turns to Pakistan Railways itself — why an institution the British built in 1861, once among Asia's strongest railway systems, declined so far. Moeez points to the Benazir incident, in which almost 250 to 260 coaches were burnt, myopic policy makers, and a department that was never a priority in any political agenda. He explains why railways can never be profit-making, why freight is the real backbone, and why 1,300 to 1,400 condemned bogies are still standing across Pakistan.

The episode closes with practical advice for aspirants — written practice over reading, why English carries 60 to 90 percent of the result, and why Dawn is the wrong newspaper to start with — and with Moeez's own definition of success: a widow's benevolent fund file, signed without delay, and the smile of a labourer whose problem got solved.

Key Takeaways

  • CSS is worth it if you want to serve in Pakistan — unless you have your own business and are earning well, there is no better option.
  • Self-belief is the most important thing in the whole CSS journey: look at your own paper, not someone else's.
  • Written practice gives you the maximum in CSS — you have already read enough, now write.
  • English carries 60 to 90 percent of your CSS result, because the essay is a test of your English, not of current affairs.
  • Dawn is not for beginners: start with the Express Tribune, build up to Dawn slowly, and keep three or four authors you follow.
  • A train carrying 1,200 passengers cannot even cover its fuel cost — railways are meant to be subsidised, and freight is their real backbone.
  • Corruption happens everywhere; what matters is what you deliver with it and what utility the public gets.
  • Success in public service is a widow's benevolent fund file signed without delay, and the smile of a labourer whose problem you solved.
  • Decide in graduation itself whether you want CSS, and choose subjects that will help you in it.

About The Guest

Moeez Haider

Frequently Asked Questions

What is CSS in Pakistan?

The Central Superior Services exam, conducted by the Federal Public Service Commission every year. It is Pakistan's most prestigious and competitive exam, and those who clear it are appointed to top administrator and leadership roles in government.

How many attempts does a CSS candidate get?

Three attempts, with an age limit of 30 years. If you do not get allocation in one attempt, you have to start again from the beginning, and that attempt is consumed.

Why did Pakistan Railways decline?

According to Moeez, several factors: the Benazir incident in which almost 250 to 260 coaches were burnt, myopic policy makers, a department that was never a political priority, and infrastructure the British laid that was never maintained or replaced.

Can Pakistan Railways make a profit?

No — like the Lahore metro, railways are meant to be subsidised, not profit-making. A train carrying 1,200 passengers often cannot even cover its fuel cost; freight, especially coal, fertiliser and phosphates, is what actually earns for the railways.

What is the most important CSS preparation tip?

Written practice: you have already read enough, so write as much as possible. And English — Moeez says it carries 60 to 90 percent of the result, because the essay tests your English, not your knowledge of current affairs.

Who is the guest in this episode?

Moeez Haider, an Assistant Transportation Officer (ATO), grade 17, in Pakistan Railways. He cleared CSS in 2022, was born and raised in Lahore, and graduated in medicine from Fatima Jinnah Medical College.
